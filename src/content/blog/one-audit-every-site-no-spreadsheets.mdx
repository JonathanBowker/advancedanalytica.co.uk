# One audit. Every site. No spreadsheets.

The estate audit is often treated as an event. It should be an operating state.

If the estate only becomes audit-ready when someone spends days assembling spreadsheets, certificates, PDFs, photographs, consultant reports, and email threads, the system is carrying hidden risk.

A modern estate should be able to generate an audit pack across every site because the evidence is already structured.

## The audit-ready estate

An AI-driven compliance layer can maintain records across asset classes and produce different views from the same evidence base.

| Asset class | Audit evidence |
|---|---|
| Residential units | Certificates, inspections, maintenance, tenant records |
| Commercial units | Lease obligations, statutory checks, service records |
| Farm buildings | Risk assessments, machinery, storage, access, safety records |
| Renewable assets | Generation, maintenance, faults, contracts |
| Woodland plots | Registration, planting, monitoring, verification evidence |
| Natural-capital projects | Baseline, intervention, monitoring, reporting, claims |

## Why spreadsheets fail

Spreadsheets are useful until they become the control system. They do not naturally preserve evidence provenance, document versions, approvals, or relationships between assets and obligations.

They are also easy to fork. Once five versions exist, nobody knows which one is authoritative.

An audit system needs a single source of truth.

## What AI adds

AI can reduce the labour of maintaining that source of truth. It can read incoming documents, identify dates, obligations, asset references, missing fields, and contradictions. It can prepare draft audit summaries and generate exception lists.

Most importantly, it can keep humans focused on issues that need judgement.

The estate team should not spend its best time copying dates from certificates into spreadsheets. It should spend that time resolving the issues the system has surfaced.

## The operating principle

Audit readiness should be continuous.

Every new document, inspection, photograph, contractor note, or consultant report should become part of the evidence model when it arrives. By the time an audit is needed, the estate should already know the answer.
