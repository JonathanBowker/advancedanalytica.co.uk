# Estate compliance fails when obligations and evidence drift apart

This case study shows how rural estate compliance can be kept continuously ready by connecting obligations, evidence, expiry dates, and escalation rules in one operating model. Estate teams often manage certificates, inspections, property-level requirements, and safety evidence across scattered files and spreadsheets, which means gaps appear only when a deadline or inspection is already close. The agent monitors readiness across properties, flags missing evidence earlier, and prepares inspection packs before they are requested.

## Challenge
- Building regulations, safety certificates, and inspection evidence were tracked across scattered spreadsheets.
- Deadlines were often discovered too late.
- Compliance status depended on manual checking across properties and document stores.
- The estate needed a live view of obligations, evidence, expiry dates, and inspection readiness.

## Approach
- Mapped property obligations, evidence requirements, renewal dates, and escalation rules into one operating model.
- Connected evidence records to the obligations they satisfied.
- Built an agent that continuously checks current obligations against available evidence.
- Flagged gaps, upcoming expiry risk, and items requiring owner action.
- Prepared inspection-ready documentation so the estate could respond before a formal request arrived.

## Outcome
- The estate moves from reactive compliance checking to continuous readiness.
- Missing or expiring evidence is flagged before it becomes a breach.
- Property teams can see compliance status across the estate without manually reconciling spreadsheets.
- Inspection preparation becomes a managed workflow instead of a last-minute search.

## Real-world example

A large rural estate needed to manage building regulation and safety evidence across many properties. We built an agent that monitors obligations, catches gaps early, and prepares the documents an inspector would need.
