# Moving brand screening from manual recall to governed production

This anonymised case study shows how a global entertainment brand turned a demanding partner-marketing review process into a controlled production pipeline. Partner submissions had to be checked for approved names and nomenclature, trademark presentation, legal notices, imagery, and other context-dependent requirements. Manual reviewers brought essential judgement, but the volume made it difficult to apply every rule consistently, at any hour, without creating a queue.

We converted the brand book into machine-readable rules and built a screening pipeline around them. Each submission is read in context, checked against the applicable rule set, and returned with correction-ready findings. Every finding contains the original content, the recommended correction, and the exact rule behind it. Final approval remains with the brand's marketing team; the system gives them a complete, evidenced first review before they begin.

## The challenge

The organisation receives partner-created marketing materials that must comply with a detailed global brand book. The review covers a dozen rule domains, including naming, trademark use, notices, imagery, and presentation requirements.

A human reviewer can apply deep experience, but cannot reliably hold every condition and exception in working memory for every submission. Review quality can vary, repetitive checks consume expert time, and work submitted outside business hours waits in a queue.

The brand needed faster feedback without handing final approval to an automated system. It also needed every correction to be explainable, so reviewers and partners could see the governing rule rather than receive an unsupported pass or fail.

## The approach

We structured the relevant brand-book requirements as machine-readable data. Rules were separated from the pages containing them and represented with the conditions required to determine when each one applied.

The pipeline:

- reads each submitted marketing asset;
- identifies the content and context requiring checks;
- applies the relevant rules across all defined domains;
- returns the original wording or asset reference;
- recommends a specific correction; and
- cites the rule supporting each finding.

The system does not replace the brand team. It prepares the submission for expert review by completing exhaustive, repeatable checks first and making exceptions easy to inspect.

## Correction-ready findings

Generic AI review often produces broad comments about making content feel more on-brand. That is not sufficient for a production approval process. The pipeline returns an actionable finding: what is wrong, what should change, and which approved rule requires that change.

Because provenance travels with the finding, a reviewer can confirm or overturn it in seconds. They no longer have to search the brand book to reconstruct the recommendation. Partners receive clearer feedback, reducing repeated interpretation and avoidable review cycles.

## Production performance

Over ten weeks, the pipeline handled hundreds of screening runs. Automated feedback was typically available within a few minutes of submission, at any hour, with no review queue.

Every submission was checked against the full applicable rule set across a dozen domains. The pipeline did not tire, skip a category, or vary its standard between reviewers. Human experts retained the final decision, focusing on judgement, exceptions, and approval rather than repeating mechanical checks.

## Outcome

The organisation gained a consistent first-line brand screening capability operating at partner-production speed. Marketing teams receive correction-ready findings before final review, partners get clearer guidance, and every recommendation arrives with evidence attached.

The operating model preserves the right division of responsibility: machines perform exhaustive and repeatable rule application, while accountable people retain authority over interpretation and final approval.

## What this case study demonstrates

Machine-readable policy can move beyond knowledge retrieval into live operational assurance. When every finding carries its source and correction, AI becomes a governed review layer rather than another opinion in the approval process.
