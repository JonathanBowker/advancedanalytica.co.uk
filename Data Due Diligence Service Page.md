# Data Due Diligence

## Know what your data contains before your AI uses it.

Data Due Diligence is the first step before any client data enters IBOM.

We examine the data, prove what it contains, prepare it, and confirm who is accountable for it.

Most firms approach data due diligence as a legal exercise. We examine the data itself and the operating context around it.

That means testing the substance against the story across systems, data, people, and process.

The result is a clear view of what exists, what can be trusted, what needs remediation, and what can enter IBOM.

## Data Due Diligence examines the data itself.

Business data can look dependable from a distance while containing structural problems, hidden exposure, and unclear ownership.

Descriptions from teams and suppliers are useful, but they aren't proof of what sits inside the data.

We establish that proof before agents depend on it.

We examine structure, cleanliness, validity, provenance, ownership, privacy exposure, security threats, and the processes surrounding each source.

We don't present this work as legal advice. Legal basis is documented as part of the evidence and referred appropriately.

## Substance against story

We compare four connected views of the data estate.

### Systems

We identify the databases, applications, stores, interfaces, and supplier feeds holding or moving relevant data.

### Data

We examine what the data contains, how it is structured, and whether it meets the required quality thresholds.

### People

We identify who owns each asset, who understands it, who changes it, and who accepts responsibility for its use.

### Process

We map how data is collected, supplied, changed, approved, corrected, transferred, and prepared for operational use.

This comparison reveals gaps between documented expectations, team understanding, supplier claims, and the data itself.

## Database intelligence from the data itself

AA’s deep dive tool gives the client database intelligence. It reads the data dictionary and schemas, then examines the data itself.

It reports three kinds of finding.

### Quality problems

These include structural and content problems, such as text sitting in the wrong columns.

### Privacy risk

These findings include personal data and potential GDPR exposure requiring review.

### Security threats

These findings include injected SQL and malicious scripts found within the examined data.

The deep dive tool produces Auditable findings. We use those findings to support decisions, ownership, remediation, and IBOM entry.

## Our nine-step process

### 1. Inventory the data estate

We inventory every system, store, and data asset, with a named owner for each.

### 2. Examine structure

We examine the data dictionary and schemas, then profile structure, types, missing values and pattern violations.

### 3. Test data quality

We test the data against five attributes: equivalence, accuracy, consistency, completeness, and integrity.

- **Equivalence:** Matching concepts and values mean the same thing across sources.
- **Accuracy:** Values represent the facts, events, or conditions they claim to represent.
- **Consistency:** Records, formats, definitions, and relationships don't contradict each other.
- **Completeness:** Required records and fields are present for their intended purpose.
- **Integrity:** Relationships, constraints, and dependencies remain intact across the data.

### 4. Scan for risk

We scan for personal data, GDPR exposure, injected SQL, malicious scripts, poor data, and stale or duplicate records.

### 5. Check legal basis and provenance

We record where the data came from, how it was obtained, and the stated basis for holding and using it.

This records the evidence for review. It doesn't replace advice from the client's legal or privacy specialists.

### 6. Reconcile substance against story

We compare what people say exists against what the deep dive tool finds in the data.

We document differences between stated controls, expected structures, supplier descriptions, and the evidence found.

### 7. Report and assign remediation

We produce a gap analysis and remediation plan covering findings, priorities, owners, dependencies, and due dates.

### 8. Prepare data for IBOM

We clean, structure, document, and govern approved data before bringing it into IBOM.

Data that doesn't meet the agreed entry conditions remains outside IBOM until remediation is complete.

### 9. Contract and monitor suppliers

We agree the data supplier contract and establish ongoing monitoring against its stated quality and control requirements.

## Accountability is named, assigned, and accepted.

Every data asset has a named owner.

Every finding is assigned to a person with a due date and a defined remediation requirement.

One named individual at the client signs off that the data is fit to enter IBOM.

Advanced Analytica is accountable for ingestion and connection into IBOM.

We are also accountable for the accuracy of findings produced by the deep dive tool.

The supplier remains accountable for the quality of the data they provide.

This model keeps ownership clear before data becomes available to people, workflows, or AI agents.

## The supplier contract protects data quality after entry.

Data Due Diligence doesn't end when a source first meets the entry conditions.

Supplier data can drift, schemas can change, and quality can fall after the initial review.

The supplier contract therefore includes:

- Quality thresholds covering equivalence, accuracy, consistency, completeness, and integrity.
- A named owner accountable for each supplied data asset.
- AA's right to re-run the deep dive tool on an agreed schedule.
- Advance notice of changes to schemas, structures, fields, or delivery formats.
- A defined remediation window when supplied data fails an agreed threshold.
- Quarantine rules preventing failing data from entering IBOM.

Failing data is quarantined and never ingested.

- A complete inventory of examined systems, stores, data assets, and named owners.
- A database intelligence report containing Auditable quality, privacy, and security findings.
- A quality assessment against equivalence, accuracy, consistency, completeness, and integrity.
- A record of provenance and the stated legal basis for each relevant source.
- A reconciliation of stakeholder claims against the evidence found in the data.
- A gap analysis with priorities, assigned owners, due dates, and remediation requirements.
- Prepared and governed data that meets the agreed conditions for entry into IBOM.
- A supplier contract defining ownership, quality thresholds, monitoring rights, and quarantine conditions.
- An agreed monitoring approach for checking that supplied data continues to meet its requirements.
